# Hybrid Perspective Gunplay

## From Third-Person Traversal to First-Person Precision

Granada has two cameras and one rule for switching between them: **move in third person, shoot in first person.**

Third person is for moving and reading the ground: fence lines, rooftops, the gap between two containers. First person is for the fight itself, when the only thing that matters is what's in your sights. This briefing covers the moment in between, when you raise your weapon and the view changes.

---

## Phase 1: Third-Person Traversal

In third person, the camera sits behind your operator's shoulder. That's where you spend most of a raid.

- **Vaulting and climbing.** You go over fences, railings, shelving and barriers in third person, so you can see where your feet land and what's on the far side before you commit.
- **Reading the ground.** The wider view shows cover, routes and exits. You plan the next move before you make it.
- **Shoulder swap.** You can move the camera to the other shoulder to match the corner you're working.

### The line-of-sight rule

The third-person camera shows **only what your operator could see with their own eyes.** If an enemy is hidden from your operator's head position, they're hidden on your screen too, even if the camera floats high enough to have a view.

That means no peeking over cover, no looking around corners and no using a wall as a free periscope. The camera helps you move. It doesn't give you information your operator wouldn't have.

### Hip-fire in third person

You can hip-fire in third person, but the shot comes from the barrel, not from the camera. If there's a crate between your muzzle and the target, the shot hits the crate, even when your crosshair looks clear. The crosshair changes to show when your barrel is blocked.

---

## Phase 2: The Transition

Press aim and the camera cuts from over your shoulder to down your sights. There's no swooping camera move to wait through.

### What carries over

- **Your aim point.** Whatever was under your third-person crosshair is under your sight when the view switches. You don't have to find the target again.
- **Your facing.** The switch never rotates you. You're looking exactly where you were.
- **Your momentum.** If you were moving, you keep moving, at ADS speed.

### What you give up

- **Peripheral vision.** First person narrows your view to what's in front of the weapon. For awareness behind and beside you, you rely on sound: footsteps, reloads and gear rattle.
- **Speed.** Movement slows while you aim.

### Settle time

The camera switch is instant, but the weapon takes a moment to settle in your hands. That settle time depends on:

- **The weapon.** A compact SMG settles faster than a heavy rifle with a long optic.
- **Your operator's condition.** Injuries and scars from earlier runs slow it down. Sable's hand tremor adds a small sway to the first half-second after a hard sprint.
- **What you were doing.** Aiming straight out of a sprint or a vault settles slower than aiming from a walk.

### Aiming mid-vault

You can't aim while vaulting or climbing. If you press aim mid-vault, the input is held and you raise your weapon the moment you land. You don't have to time a second press.

---

## Phase 3: First-Person Precision

Once you're down your sights, the fight is as direct as any first-person shooter.

- **What you see is what you hit.** Your eye is on the sight and the shot comes from the barrel. There's no third-person offset between the two.
- **Recoil and sway.** Recoil is controlled with your aim, and sway changes with stance, stamina and injuries.
- **Breath hold.** Holding your breath steadies a long shot for a few seconds, at a cost to stamina.
- **Scars in frame.** Wounds your operator carries show through the sight, such as a burned forearm, a patched glove or a cracked respirator seal.

---

## Phase 4: Breaking Contact

Release aim and the camera pulls back over your shoulder. The line-of-sight rule applies again straight away. Anyone you can no longer see from your operator's position disappears from your screen too.

This is when you move: pick your next cover, find the exit or push. Then raise your weapon again when the next fight starts.

---

## What Each Camera Can and Can't Do

| | Third Person | First Person (ADS) |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Movement, vaulting, reading the ground | Close-quarters and precision shooting |
| **Can see** | Only what your operator can see | Only what's in front of your weapon |
| **Can peek around cover?** | No | No |
| **Where shots come from** | The barrel | The barrel |
| **Movement speed** | Full | Reduced |

## Field Questions

### Is Granada first-person or third-person?
Both. You move and scout in third person and switch to first person when you aim down sights.

### Can I use third person to peek around cover?
No. The third-person camera shows only what your operator could see from their own head position, so it can't be used to look over cover or around corners.

### Is there a delay when switching to first person?
The camera switch is instant. After that, the weapon takes a moment to settle, depending on the weapon, what you were doing and any injuries your operator carries.

### Where do shots come from in third person?
From the barrel. If cover is between your muzzle and the target, the shot hits the cover, and the crosshair shows when your barrel is blocked.

### Do injuries affect aiming?
Yes. Scars and injuries carried between runs can slow weapon settle time and add sway.

# Granada Stash & Progression Protocol

## Why We Rejected Pay-to-Win Editions

An extraction shooter asks one question every raid: **what are you willing to lose?**

Every system in the genre hangs off that question. The gear you bring in, the route you take, the fight you walk away from, the moment you decide to leave. When a studio sells stash space and secure containers in editions that cost $100 to $250, it changes the answer for some players and not others. The raid stops being a fair bet.

We decided early that Granada would never sell an edition like that. This page explains why, and what we built instead.

---

## What Paid Stash Actually Breaks

### 1. It turns risk into a budget line

Stash space is how much you can afford to lose. A player with double the room can hoard spare kits, run expensive loadouts without worrying, and absorb a string of deaths that would wipe out a standard player. Two people in the same raid are no longer taking the same risk. One of them paid to make losing cheaper.

### 2. A bought secure container undercuts the extract

A secure container keeps items safe even if you die. When a premium edition ships with a larger one, the paying player keeps more of every raid no matter how it ends. The tension of the walk to extract, the part where you decide whether that last room is worth it, matters less for them than for everyone else. The core loop gets softer for the people who spent more.

### 3. It makes the economy about money, not decisions

More storage means more to sell, more to craft with and more to trade. A head start in space turns into a head start in currency, which turns into better gear, which turns into more wins. It compounds. By the time a standard player has earned their way to the same footing, the paying player is already miles ahead.

### 4. It poisons trust

When every advantage might have been bought, nobody's win means much. Players stop asking "how did they survive that?" and start asking "what edition are they on?" That suspicion hurts a genre built on earned reputation.

## The Modular Rig System

Granada's answer is simple: **your stash grows only by what you pull out of the Zone.**

### How it works

1. **Every player starts with the same base stash and the same small sealed case.** There's no edition, bundle or pre-order that changes this.
2. **Industrial scrap spawns in the Sector 4 bunkers.** The old maintenance levels under the collapsed sector hold salvage: pipe fittings, rail brackets, pressure-rated sheet steel, damaged servo housings.
3. **Scrap has to be extracted.** If you die carrying it, it's gone, the same as any other loot. It doesn't go into your sealed case; it takes up real bag space and slows you down.
4. **Back at your base, the Workbench turns scrap into rig modules.** Each module adds a fixed block of stash grid, or upgrades your sealed case one step.
5. **Modules snap onto your rig frame.** The frame has a set number of mounting points, and higher tiers of scrap unlock stronger modules.

### Why Sector 4

The bunkers are the most contested ground in the Zone. They're deep, poorly lit and there's only one way out of each level. Scrap sits where the risk is highest on purpose. Growing your stash means going somewhere dangerous and coming back.

### What you can't do

- **Buy scrap, modules or rig frames with real money.** There is no store listing for them.
- **Skip the Workbench.** Every expansion is built from scrap you extracted yourself.
- **Keep scrap you didn't get out.** Die in the bunker and your haul stays there for someone else.

### What we may sell

Granada is one edition at one price. If we ever sell anything after launch, it will be cosmetic only: things that change how you look, not what you can carry, keep or survive.

## The Comparison

| | Paid-Edition Extraction Shooters | Granada |
|---|---|---|
| **Edition price** | Base game plus editions from ~$100 to $250 | One edition, one price |
| **Stash size** | Larger stash bundled with higher editions | Same base stash for all; grown through Modular Rig modules |
| **Secure container** | Bigger container bundled with higher editions | Same sealed case for all; upgraded with extracted scrap |
| **How expansions are earned** | Purchased | Scrap extracted from Sector 4 bunkers, built at the Workbench |
| **Can advantage be bought later?** | Often, through new editions | No. Cosmetics only |

## Field Questions

### Does Granada sell stash space or secure container upgrades?
No. Stash space and sealed case upgrades come only from rig modules built with industrial scrap you extract from the Sector 4 bunkers.

### Is there a deluxe or premium edition?
No. Granada has one edition at one price, and every player starts with the same stash and sealed case.

### How do I expand my stash?
Extract industrial scrap from the Sector 4 bunkers, bring it back to your base and build rig modules at the Workbench. Each module adds stash space or upgrades your sealed case.

### What happens to scrap if I die in a raid?
You lose it. Scrap can't be stored in your sealed case, so it only counts once you extract with it.

### Will Granada sell anything after launch?
If we sell anything after launch, it will be cosmetic only. Nothing sold will affect stash size, gear, secure storage or combat.

### Can new players catch up with veterans?
Yes. Everyone starts with the same base stash, and nobody can buy their way ahead. Veterans are ahead only because they extracted more.

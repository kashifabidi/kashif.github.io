# SABLE — Vance Korhonen

**Clearance:** Declassified, partial
**Last known position:** Sector 4 outer perimeter, Gate 11
**Status:** Active. Refuses extraction.

Vance Korhonen was a perimeter-maintenance lead at the Granada Exclusion Zone. He ran the fence crews, the sensor masts and the gate locks. When Sector 4 went down, he was the last man with working keys to Gate 11. He still has them.

---

## Intercept Log 4-11-A

> *Recovered from a damaged relay mast, Sector 4 north fence. Audio partially corrupted. Transcribed verbatim. Speaker identified by voiceprint as V. KORHONEN.*

**[00:00:04]** — Relay's up. Don't know who's listening. Doesn't matter.

**[00:00:11]** — Sector 4 didn't fall. People keep saying it fell, like a wall. It didn't fall. It leaked. The coolant loop under Block C went first, then the scrubbers, then the sirens came nine minutes late because someone upstairs wanted a second reading.

**[00:00:37]** — Nine minutes. That's how long the Sector 4 day shift had. Two hundred and six people on the board. I counted the ones who reached Gate 11. Forty-one.

**[00:00:58]** — Command sealed the inner line at hour two. Protocol. I understand protocol. I wrote half of it. But you seal a line, you're telling everyone behind it that the door is shut.

**[00:01:20]** — *[static — 6 seconds]*

**[00:01:26]** — ...still pinging. Three badge beacons, deep in Block C. They move. Not much. But they move. The beacons are supposed to go flat when the wearer does. They haven't gone flat.

**[00:01:44]** — So no, I'm not taking the lift out. I've got the keys to Gate 11, a working mast, and enough filters for eleven days if I'm careful. The gate stays open from this side. If anyone walks out of Sector 4, somebody's going to be standing here when they do.

**[00:02:03]** — My respirator's cracked on the left seal. Burns a little when I breathe deep. My hand still shakes from the Block C door. None of that matters.

**[00:02:15]** — If you're one of the ones who got out — don't come back for me. Come back for them.

**[00:02:21]** — *[transmission ends]*

## Scars and Wear

Sable's body is a record of the runs he has survived. Nothing on this list resets.

- **Cracked left respirator seal.** It lets in trace contamination during long sprints, so his stamina recovers more slowly in heavy-exposure zones until the seal is repaired with parts from a raid.
- **Tremor in the right hand.** He got it forcing the Block C blast door. There's a small sway in the first half-second of ADS after heavy exertion, and it settles if he breathes and holds.
- **Burn across the right forearm.** It's shown on his model and in his first-person view whenever he raises a weapon.
- **Psychological stake: the beacons.** The three Block C beacons are a thread that runs through the story episodes. What Sable does about them, and whether they're still moving, depends on what happens across your runs.

## Perspective Breakdown: How Sable Fights

Granada uses a hybrid camera. You move and read the ground in third person, and you aim in first person. Sable's kit and scars are built around that split. The full system is covered in [Hybrid Perspective Gunplay](/zone-briefings/hybrid-perspective-gunplay/).

### Third person: moving and reading the ground

- **Vaulting.** Sable vaults fence lines, catwalk rails, collapsed shelving and gate barriers in third person, so you can see his footing and the drop on the far side before you commit.
- **Spatial awareness.** The pulled-back camera shows rooflines, container gaps and the next patch of cover. You plan a route before you run it.
- **Line-of-sight rule.** The third-person camera shows only what Sable himself could see. If an enemy is hidden from his eyes, your screen won't show them either. The camera can't be used to peek over cover or around corners.

### First person: close-quarters shooting

- **Instant sight alignment.** Aiming down sights snaps the view straight into first person through Sable's optic. There's no transition animation to wait through in a doorway fight.
- **What you see is what hits.** In first person, the shot comes from the barrel and your eye is on the sight. Third-person offset can't make you miss on a corner.
- **Scars in frame.** His burned forearm and the half-second tremor after a sprint are visible through the optic. Breathe, hold, then fire.
- **Drop out and reset.** Release ADS and the camera pulls back to third person, so you can read the room again after contact.
